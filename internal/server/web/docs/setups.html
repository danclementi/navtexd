Typical channel setups

The settings that matter for the most common ways of feeding navtexd. Everything is set under the Options tab in the Channel sub-tabs. Option names {like_this} are the variable names shown there, along with the default values of this version in parentheses.

Before you start

  • Each channel has its own frequency selector {frequency_hz} and it's own source controls. The first control {shared_from}sets whether the channel will share the source of another channel or will have its own. The second control {source} selects the type of source (if it has its own).
  • The decoder needs an approximate point to look for the NAVTEX carrier. It gets this hint from the parameter{lo_below_hz}. This represents how far below the carrier the receiver's dial is (in the case of real audio), or how far below the carrier the center frequency of the I/Q stream (I/Q) is. The automatic search limit {search_range_hz}sets the maximum range of the searchfrom the expected carrier frequency. Leave input.*.autotune on: the carrier is then found and followed automatically.
  • You need not choose USB or LSB: the polarity can be detected automatically (decoder.polarity = auto), and no sample-rate calibration is needed.
  • How to see that it works. When a signal is available in a channel, click on its spectrum "thumbnail" at the top of the display; this selects that channel for viewing. You may then open the Spectrum tab for a detailed view. The carrier marker sits between two peaks 170 Hz apart; the orange trace (the signal after the channel filter) follows the signal; and the LOCK, SYNC and DATA indicators of the channel come on.

1. Real-valued audio (a receiver's audio output)

A conventional receiver in USB mode, with its audio going to a sound card or a virtual audio cable. The signal appears as a pair of tones in the audio.

OptionTypical valueNotes
input.sourcedeviceSound card or virtual audio cable
input.device.namepart of the device nameEmpty = the system default input. The program's -list-devices command lists the names.
input.device.channels11 = real audio, 2 = I/Q
input.device.sample_rate_hz48000Sound-card rates of 8 to 48 kHz are possible; 48000 is typical
input.device.lo_below_hz1000The receiver's dial is 1 kHz below the channel
channel.frequency_hz518000The NAVTEX channel
  • Dial the receiver to the channel minus input.device.lo_below_hz in USB: 517.000 kHz for 518 kHz, 489.000 kHz for 490 kHz. The carrier then lies at 1000 Hz in the audio. Keep it between about 0.5 and 2.5 kHz, away from 0 Hz and the top of the band.
  • LSB: if the receiver is in LSB, the dial is above the channel: give input.device.lo_below_hz a negative value (−1000, dial 519.000 kHz). The two tones are then swapped, which the polarity detection handles.
  • One channel per audio stream. A narrow audio channel cannot hold both 518 and 490 kHz. For two channels use two audio inputs (two receivers, or two virtual cables), each channel with its own input.device.name.
  • Receiver filter: a USB passband of about 3 kHz is plenty for a carrier at 1000 Hz.
  • A stereo I/Q signal from a sound card (for example an SDR program's I/Q output over a virtual cable) uses the same options with input.device.channels = 2 and a higher sample rate; if left and right are exchanged, use input.device.swap_iq.

2. Two channels (518 and 490 kHz) from one I/Q stream

A wideband I/Q stream holds both NAVTEX frequencies. One program opens it once, and both channels decode from the same samples, each with its own down-converter, carrier and settings.

ChannelOptionValue
Channel 1channel.frequency_hz518000
Channel 1input.shared_from0 (own input: it opens the stream)
Channel 1input.sourcespyserver or sdrconnect (see sections 3 and 4)
Channel 2channel.frequency_hz490000
Channel 2input.shared_from1 (“same input as Channel 1”)
  • The stream's centre is set by Channel 1: channel 1's frequency minus its lo_below_hz. With the default of 1000 Hz and 518 kHz, the centre is 517 kHz and the 490 kHz carrier is 27 kHz below it.
  • The span of the stream must hold both carriers with a margin of several kHz at each side. The 250 kS/s of SDRconnect and the 228 kS/s of SpyServer (±125 and ±114 kHz) hold 518 and 490 kHz easily; 424 kHz would be at the very edge.
  • The owner (channel 1) must be on and have an input of its own; a sharing channel cannot itself be shared. Restarting the owner restarts the channels that share it.
  • Channel 2 has no input options of its own. Its other settings (framing, filter, receiver options) are independent, so the two channels can also decode the same frequency with different settings: give both the same channel.frequency_hz and compare them (see the Compare options).
  • OpenHPSDR radios work differently: there each channel gets a receiver of its own inside the radio (section 5), so you do not use input.shared_from.

3. Airspy through SpyServer

Airspy's SpyServer program owns the Airspy (HF+ Discovery, Airspy One, RTL-SDR) and streams I/Q over TCP. It can run on the same computer, or on another computer or a Raspberry Pi on your network.

OptionTypical valueNotes
input.sourcespyserver
input.spyserver.addresshost:portEmpty = 127.0.0.1:5555, SpyServer on this computer
input.spyserver.gain−1−1 leaves the server's setting. Otherwise a gain index from 0 up to the receiver's maximum.
input.spyserver.lo_below_hz1000The I/Q stream is centred 1 kHz below the channel. A few kHz moves the carrier away from the receiver's DC spur.
input.spyserver.swap_iqfalseSwitch on only if the spectrum is mirrored (see section 5)
input.spyserver.autotunetrue
  • The sample rate is not an option: the decimation stage nearest to 250 kS/s is used (the HF+ Discovery: 228 kS/s, ±114 kHz around the centre).
  • Start SpyServer first and check that it is streaming. The program tunes the receiver to the centre it needs; it only receives.
  • Adjust the gain until INP.LEVEL stays out of the red on the strongest signals.
  • To decode 518 and 490 kHz from the same stream, add the second channel as in section 2.

4. SDRplay through SDRconnect (headless)

SDRconnect (version 1.0.9 or later), with its window or headless, owns the SDRplay receiver. navtexd is a client of SDRconnect's WebSocket server and asks it for the I/Q stream. Enable the WebSocket server in SDRconnect (Preferences, WebSocket Server); by default it listens on port 5454.

OptionTypical valueNotes
input.sourcesdrconnect
input.sdrconnect.addresshost:portEmpty = 127.0.0.1:5454, SDRconnect on this computer
input.sdrconnect.devicee.g. RSPduoPart of the receiver's name or its serial number. Empty = the receiver SDRconnect has selected.
input.sdrconnect.antennae.g. Tuner 1 50 ohm, HiZ PortAs SDRconnect names it; a unique part is enough. Empty = leave as it is.
input.sdrconnect.lna_state−1−1 leaves the receiver's setting. Otherwise the gain-reduction step.
input.sdrconnect.lo_below_hz1000The receiver is tuned 1 kHz below the channel
input.sdrconnect.swap_iqfalse
input.sdrconnect.autotunetrue
  • The sample rate is fixed at 250 kS/s (±125 kHz around the centre).
  • Only one program should control the receiver. If another client changes the sample rate, the stream stops with an explanation, and a changed centre frequency is logged as a warning because the carrier is no longer where the decoder looks.
  • Adjust input.sdrconnect.lna_state (or the gain in SDRconnect) until INP.LEVEL stays out of the red.
  • To decode 518 and 490 kHz from the same stream, add the second channel as in section 2.

5. Hermes and other OpenHPSDR radios

I/Q straight from an OpenHPSDR (Protocol 1) radio over the network: Hermes, Hermes Lite, ANAN and Pavel Demin's Red Pitaya and QMtech images. Receive only. Close Thetis, SDRConnect or any other program that uses the radio first, because only one program can use a radio address at a time.

OptionTypical valueNotes
input.sourcehpsdr
input.hpsdr.addresse.g. 192.168.1.50Empty = search the local network. The radios that worked last are offered in a list.
input.hpsdr.sample_rate_hz4800048000, 96000, 192000 or 384000. 48 kS/s is enough for one channel.
input.hpsdr.lo_below_hz3000The radio is tuned 3 kHz below the channel, so the carrier sits 3 kHz from the radio's DC spur
input.hpsdr.mirror_iqfalseLeave off; see below
input.hpsdr.autotunetrue
  • Several channels from one radio: do not use input.shared_from. Give each channel input.source = hpsdr with the same address and the same sample rate. The radio is asked for one receiver per channel, each tuned to its own channel, so 518, 490 and 424 kHz can all come from one radio and one antenna (a radio offers up to 8 receivers).
  • Each receiver delivers its own I/Q stream, so 48 kS/s is enough for every channel.
  • Watch the System Log for lost packets on a busy network.

If no carrier is found: mirror_iq

The program already exchanges I and Q for OpenHPSDR radios, because every radio tried so far (Red Pitaya and QMtech with Pavel Demin's images, Hermes, Hermes Lite) needs it to give the right spectrum. If a radio of yours still comes out mirrored, the two tones swap places, which the polarity detection handles, but the carrier also appears on the other side of the centre, at −input.hpsdr.lo_below_hz instead of +, outside the place where the search looks. The symptom is a clean signal in the spectrum, on the wrong side of the centre, but no LOCK. Switch input.hpsdr.mirror_iq on, and the channel restarts. The other I/Q inputs have the usual swap_iq option (input.device.swap_iq, input.sdrconnect.swap_iq, input.spyserver.swap_iq).
